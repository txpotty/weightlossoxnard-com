    How Wellness & Longevity Care Works in Santa Barbara

    Wellness and longevity care here means seven specific treatments, each tied to a goal such as chronic pain, a suspected parasitic infection, or interest in research-informed peptides. The first step is naming the goal and the symptoms behind it. Evidence comes next. Some options are FDA-approved drugs used off-label, and others are compounded and not FDA-approved. Primary care stays in the picture.

    Low Dose Naltrexone

    Naltrexone is FDA-approved at 50 mg for alcohol and opioid use disorders, while low dose naltrexone, usually 1.5 to 4.5 mg at bedtime, is prescribed off-label. It is thought to briefly block opioid receptors and calm inflammatory signaling in the nervous system. Studies are small. Trials have looked at fibromyalgia and some autoimmune conditions, with mixed but encouraging results. It cannot be taken with opioids.

    Naltrexone Gum in Santa Barbara

    Naltrexone gum is a compounded form that delivers a small dose through the lining of the mouth, and some providers use it as a flexible, as-needed option to reduce cravings in specific situations. It is not FDA-approved. Research is limited. You will get specific directions on timing and use. Opioid use and liver problems are screened first.

    Antiparasitic Protocol, Ivermectin and Mebendazole

    Ivermectin is FDA-approved to treat certain parasitic worm infections such as strongyloidiasis and onchocerciasis, and it is also used for scabies and head lice. Mebendazole treats intestinal worms such as pinworm, roundworm, whipworm and hookworm. Travel history matters. The Antiparasitic Protocol is prescribed when symptoms, travel or exposure suggest an infection, with dosing based on the parasite. These are prescribed only for their anti-parasitic uses.

    Anti-Aging and Immune Support Peptides in Santa Barbara

    Peptides are short chains of amino acids that act as signaling molecules, and our Anti-Aging Peptides and Immune Support Peptides are compounded, research-informed therapies that are not FDA-approved for these uses. Evidence ranges from early human studies to mostly laboratory research. Most are injected. Expect a tiny needle under the skin a few times a week. FDA rules on compounded peptides can change availability.

    How a Provider Chooses Between Options

    Chronic pain or inflammation may point toward low dose naltrexone, while a suspected parasitic infection based on symptoms, travel or exposure points toward ivermectin, mebendazole or the Antiparasitic Protocol. Peptides may be discussed for longevity or immune goals when you understand the evidence is still developing. Safety comes first. Labs or in-person care may be suggested before any prescription. Goal decides the starting point.

    Side Effects and Safety in Santa Barbara

    Low dose naltrexone may cause vivid dreams, insomnia or headache in the first weeks, which often ease or improve with morning dosing. Ivermectin can cause dizziness, nausea or itching, and mebendazole may cause stomach pain or diarrhea. Peptides vary. Expect possible redness at the injection site, plus peptide-specific effects your provider explains. Report anything unusual.

    Eligibility and Screening

    Your provider asks about your goal, symptoms, travel, opioid use, alcohol, liver and kidney health, pregnancy and every medication you take before recommending anything. Any opioid use, including tramadol or codeine, rules out naltrexone because it can trigger sudden withdrawal. Pregnancy changes options. Antiparasitics are usually avoided during pregnancy. Liver disease needs a closer look.
